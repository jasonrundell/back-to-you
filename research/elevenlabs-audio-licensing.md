# Can ElevenLabs-generated audio be redistributed in a public repo?

Research for [issue #3](https://github.com/jasonrundell/claude-code-sound-hooks/issues/3). Feeds the LICENSE decision.

**Scope:** the 15 voice-over MP3s under `sounds/claude/`, generated with ElevenLabs
Voice Design + Eleven v3 Text to Speech (see `ELEVENLABS-VOICE-PROMPT.md`), about to be
committed to a public repo and bundled into a downloadable release zip.

**Sources:** ElevenLabs' own documents only — Terms of Service, Prohibited Use Policy,
Help Center, pricing page, product docs, and their engineering blog. Every claim below
links to the document that owns it. Precedent examples are read from the actual
`LICENSE` files in the repos cited, not from write-ups about them.

**Date of research:** 2026-07-27. ElevenLabs' Prohibited Use Policy is stamped
"Last Updated 3 September 2025". Re-check before relying on this months from now.

**Not legal advice.** This is a documentation review.

---

## TL;DR

1. You own the audio. Ownership is identical on every tier — free and paid.
   ([ToS §4(c)(ii)](https://elevenlabs.io/terms-of-use))
2. Redistributing it as loose files in a public repo, and in a release zip, is
   **permitted** for Text-to-Speech output. The only clause in ElevenLabs' terms that
   bans standalone file redistribution is scoped exclusively to their *Sound Effects*
   product, which this pack does not use.
   ([Prohibited Use Policy §9(c)](https://elevenlabs.io/use-policy))
3. **Licensing those files under MIT is a breach of your agreement with ElevenLabs.**
   PUP §9(n) forbids making Output available to end users "on terms that are less
   restrictive or more permissive than the terms under which our Services and their
   Output have been made available to you." MIT is strictly more permissive.
   ([PUP §9(n)](https://elevenlabs.io/use-policy))
4. So would CC BY 4.0, CC BY-SA, or CC0. This is not a "just use Creative Commons for
   the assets" situation. Any OSI-open or CC license permits AI training, which
   PUP §9(k) and §9(l) forbid.
5. **Upgrading the plan tier does not fix this.** Paid tier fixes commercial use and
   attribution. It does nothing to §9(n)/(j)/(k)/(l).
6. One blocking unknown: **which plan tier were these generated on?** If free, the clips
   cannot ship at all in their current form and must be regenerated. `ELEVENLABS-VOICE-PROMPT.md`
   does not record it.

## 1. Who owns the audio, and does that differ by tier?

**You do, on every tier.** ElevenLabs' Terms of Service, §4(c)(ii):

> For the avoidance of doubt, Output may be generated by, but does not include,
> ElevenLabs' foundational and other artificial intelligence voice models (the "Voice
> Models") or ElevenLabs' foundational and other artificial intelligence or language
> learning models ("LLMs") (Voice Models and LLMs, collectively, the "Models"). Except
> as expressly set forth herein, as between you and ElevenLabs, you retain all rights
> in and to your Output.

— [ElevenLabs Terms of Service](https://elevenlabs.io/terms-of-use)

That clause carries no tier qualifier. Ownership is the same for a free user and a
Business user.

**What differs by tier is the licence to *use* what you own**, which is a separate axis.
ToS §1(c):

> (i) if you access or use our Services free of charge (such a user, a "Free User"), you
> may only use the Services for non-commercial purposes; (ii) if you access or use our
> Services through a paid subscription plan (such a user, a "Paid User"), you may use the
> Services for commercial purposes, but in either case, your access and use of the
> Services and any Output must still comply with the Prohibited Use Policy.

— [ElevenLabs Terms of Service §1(c)](https://elevenlabs.io/terms-of-use)

Reinforced in the Prohibited Use Policy §9(a), which lists as a prohibited use:

> If you are a free user, using our Services for any commercial purpose, including for
> advertising or running pyramid schemes, contests, or sweepstakes.

— [ElevenLabs Prohibited Use Policy](https://elevenlabs.io/use-policy)

And on the pricing page, the plan comparison table carries a feature row keyed
`commercial_license` → "Commercial License", listed under the Starter tier's
"Everything in Free, plus" block. There is a separate comparison row keyed
`no_attribution` → "No Attribution Required".
— [ElevenLabs pricing](https://elevenlabs.io/pricing)

The commercial licence **survives cancellation**. Help Center:

> Once your subscription ends, you will still have a commercial license to use whatever
> you generated during that subscription forever. However, audio generated outside of a
> subscription will always require attribution.

— [What happens to my content after my subscription ends?](https://help.elevenlabs.io/hc/en-us/articles/15993008593297-What-happens-to-my-content-after-my-subscription-ends)

So the question is not "is the account paid *now*" — it is "was the account paid **at
the moment each clip was generated**".

### The Voice Design angle

The voice here was built with **Voice Design** (text prompt → new voice), not pulled from
the Voice Library. That is the favourable case: there is no third-party voice owner in the
chain, so the [Voice Library Addendum](https://elevenlabs.io/vla) — which governs voices
other users have shared, and which lets a voice owner withdraw a voice — does not apply to
this pack. The VLA's own carve-out confirms that even withdrawal wouldn't retroactively
kill generated audio:

> any Outputs generated using your User Voice Model prior to the end of the Notice Period
> will continue to exist and remain available for use thereafter.

— [ElevenLabs Voice Library Addendum](https://elevenlabs.io/vla)

Not applicable here, but worth recording so nobody re-opens it later.

## 2. Does redistributing files differ from using the audio in a published work?

**For Text to Speech, no — both are permitted.** This is the question that most people
assume cuts against them, and the terms actually come out the other way.

ToS §4(a) expressly contemplates taking Output off-platform:

> We may enable you to download Output from some (but not all) of the Services; in such
> cases, you are permitted to use such Output outside of the Services but always subject
> to these Terms and our Prohibited Use Policy.

— [ElevenLabs Terms of Service §4(a)](https://elevenlabs.io/terms-of-use)

The Prohibited Use Policy contains exactly one clause restricting standalone
redistribution of audio files, and it is scoped to a single product — **Sound Effects**:

> c) Selling, reselling, renting, leasing, loaning, assigning, distributing, performing,
> licensing, sublicensing or commercially using or exploiting any Output (or any portion
> thereof) generated using our **Sound Effects** product on a standalone basis for any
> purpose, including as isolated files, audio samples, music or sound, libraries, or other
> collections of sounds.

— [ElevenLabs Prohibited Use Policy §9(c)](https://elevenlabs.io/use-policy) (emphasis added)

That clause describes precisely what this repo does — shipping isolated files as a
collection of sounds — and it deliberately does not reach Text to Speech Output. The same
policy's §9(b) also goes out of its way to say the anti-resale clause targets the
*Services*, not the Output:

> b) Selling, reselling, renting, leasing, loaning, assigning, licensing, or sub-licensing
> our Services without our prior written authorization. For the avoidance of doubt, this
> does not preclude your use of Output in accordance with the applicable terms and
> conditions.

— [ElevenLabs Prohibited Use Policy §9(b)](https://elevenlabs.io/use-policy)

**Conclusion:** shipping the 15 TTS clips as files in a repo is not, on its own, a
prohibited act. The problem is not *whether* you distribute them; it is *on what terms*
(section 4 below).

**Hard warning for later:** if anyone ever swaps in clips from ElevenLabs' **Sound
Effects** product — which is a natural thing to reach for when adding blips and chimes to
a sound-hooks repo — §9(c) flatly prohibits shipping them as a sound pack. No licensing
manoeuvre fixes that; it is a use restriction, not a terms mismatch. Today's chiptune pack
is safe on this axis because it is generated locally by `tools/New-ChiptuneSounds.ps1`,
not by ElevenLabs.

## 3. Does the release zip change anything?

**No.** Nothing in the ToS or PUP distinguishes a file committed to a repo from the same
file inside a downloadable archive. ToS §4(a) grants "use such Output outside of the
Services" without qualifying the container. §9(c)'s language about "isolated files… or
other collections of sounds" is the only place ElevenLabs contemplates packaged
distribution at all, and it is Sound-Effects-only.

Repo checkout and release zip stand or fall together. Whatever terms you attach to
`sounds/claude/**` must be reproduced inside the zip — a `LICENSE` file that only exists
in the git tree does not travel with an archive built from a subset of paths.

## 4. Do the terms restrict downstream users in a way that conflicts with MIT?

**Yes. This is the finding that decides the ticket.**

Four clauses in the Prohibited Use Policy are irreconcilable with an MIT grant over the
audio:

> j) Using any part of our Services or their Output to research and develop products,
> models, or services that compete with ElevenLabs, or otherwise compete with ElevenLabs.
>
> k) Using any part of our Services or their Output as input for any machine learning or
> training of artificial intelligence models.
>
> l) Using any part of our Services or their Output as part of a dataset that may be used
> for training, fine-tuning, developing, testing, or improving any machine learning or
> artificial intelligence technology.
>
> n) Making any B2B2B (Business-to-Business-to-Business), B2B2C
> (Business-to-Business-to-Consumer), or other similar use of our Services or their Output
> available to your end users **on terms that are less restrictive or more permissive than
> the terms under which our Services and their Output have been made available to you.**

— [ElevenLabs Prohibited Use Policy §9](https://elevenlabs.io/use-policy) (emphasis added)

The MIT licence grants, without limitation, permission "to use, copy, modify, merge,
publish, distribute, sublicense, and/or sell copies of the Software". It carves out no
AI-training exception, no anti-competition exception, and imposes no downstream
conditions beyond preserving the notice. Placing the MP3s under MIT is therefore an act
of handing end users terms strictly more permissive than your own — the exact thing
§9(n) names. §9(k) and §9(l) are violated the moment a downstream user does the thing
MIT invites them to do.

The PUP's scope clause makes clear this is not limited to what happens inside the
ElevenLabs product:

> This Prohibited Use Policy ("Policy") supplements the ElevenLabs Terms of Service
> ("Terms of Service") and applies to your access and use of our Services, including any
> Inputs you provide and Outputs you create. It also applies to your use within and
> outside our Website and our Services, whether directly or indirectly, as well as any
> attempts to engage in such use.

— [ElevenLabs Prohibited Use Policy](https://elevenlabs.io/use-policy)

### The nuance worth being precise about

The PUP binds **you** contractually. It does not bind a stranger who clones the repo and
never agreed to ElevenLabs' terms — and the policy says so itself:

> This Policy does not create any right or private right of action on the part of any
> third party…

— [ElevenLabs Prohibited Use Policy, Enforcement](https://elevenlabs.io/use-policy)

That is *not* a loophole. It relocates the exposure rather than removing it. Under
§9(n) the breach is **your act of granting** permissive terms, complete at the moment you
publish an MIT-licensed audio directory. You do not need a downstream user to actually
train a model on `vo-done.mp3` for the breach to exist.

There is a second, structural problem. Because §9(n) means you cannot lawfully grant
MIT-grade rights, an MIT `LICENSE` covering the whole repo would be a licence you are
not in a position to give — a defective grant to every user who relies on it. The
cleanest reading: you own the copyright and *could* grant MIT as a matter of copyright
law, but doing so breaches your contract with ElevenLabs. Contributors and redistributors
downstream inherit an asset whose stated licence and whose actual provenance disagree.
That is precisely the ambiguity an open-source LICENSE file exists to eliminate.

### Consequence: Creative Commons does not rescue this either

The reflexive fix — "MIT for code, CC BY 4.0 for assets" — fails for the same reason.
CC BY 4.0 permits commercial use, permits derivatives, and imposes no restriction on
text-and-data mining or model training; its only condition is attribution. That is still
"less restrictive or more permissive" than ElevenLabs' terms. CC BY-SA and CC0 are worse
on this axis, not better.

**The audio cannot be OSI-open or CC-licensed while it stays ElevenLabs-generated.** Any
compliant asset licence must carry ElevenLabs' restrictions forward, which by definition
makes it a non-free licence.

## 5. Is attribution required, and in what form?

**Depends entirely on the tier at generation time.** Help Center, verbatim and in full:

> The free plan does not include a commercial license and cannot be used for any
> commercial purpose. If you publish content generated using our Services on a free plan
> or without being signed-in to your account, you must attribute it to ElevenLabs by
> including "elevenlabs.io" or "11.ai" in the title (except in the case where your content
> is generated using Eleven Music on a free plan or without being signed-in to your
> account, you must attribute it to ElevenLabs by referencing "Eleven Music" when
> distributing such content). All paid plans include a commercial license, provided you're
> not using Beta Services. As long as you hold the necessary intellectual property rights
> to your generated content, and it complies with applicable laws, our Terms of Service
> and Prohibited Use Policy, you may use it commercially. Attribution requirement is
> subject to the agreement you have with ElevenLabs. For full details, refer to our Terms
> of Service. In summary:
>
> - Content generated during a paid subscription can be used commercially, and
>   indefinitely. Attribution requirement is subject to the agreement you have with
>   ElevenLabs.
> - Content created outside of a paid subscription (before or after) cannot be used
>   commercially and always requires attribution when shared non-commercially.
> - Under our Beta Services Addendum, content generated using Beta Services cannot be used
>   for any commercial purpose or in any production environment.

— [Can I publish the content I generate on the platform?](https://help.elevenlabs.io/hc/en-us/articles/13313564601361-Can-I-publish-the-content-I-generate-on-the-platform)

Read against the pricing page's `no_attribution` → "No Attribution Required" comparison
row ([pricing](https://elevenlabs.io/pricing)), the picture is:

| | Commercial use | Attribution |
|---|---|---|
| **Free / signed-out** | Prohibited | Required — `"elevenlabs.io"` or `"11.ai"` **in the title** |
| **Paid (Starter and up)** | Permitted, permanently, for anything generated during the sub | Not required |

Note the placement requirement is unusually specific: **in the title**, not in a credits
file. For a GitHub repo that would mean the repo name or release title, which is a
significant imposition. This alone is a strong argument for confirming paid-tier
provenance rather than living with the free-tier obligation.

There is no attribution clause anywhere in the [Terms of Service](https://elevenlabs.io/terms-of-use)
itself — a full-text read turns up no instance of "attribution" or "attribute". The
obligation lives only in the Help Center and the pricing page. Worth knowing if the
requirement is ever contested.

**Regardless of obligation, attribution is good practice here** and costs nothing. Even
on a paid tier, documenting provenance is what makes the asset licence defensible to a
downstream user (see section 6).

## 6. Is a split licence the standard pattern? Real examples.

**Yes — separate licences for code and non-code assets is long-established, and the
mechanics are well-trodden.** Verified by reading each project's actual licence files:

**Twemoji** — the cleanest template for this repo's shape. Two licence files at the root,
plus an unambiguous README statement:

> Code licensed under the MIT License: <http://opensource.org/licenses/MIT>
>
> Graphics licensed under CC-BY 4.0: <https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/>

— [jdecked/twemoji README.md](https://github.com/jdecked/twemoji/blob/main/README.md),
with [LICENSE](https://github.com/jdecked/twemoji/blob/main/LICENSE) and
[LICENSE-GRAPHICS](https://github.com/jdecked/twemoji/blob/main/LICENSE-GRAPHICS)

**Font Awesome** — a three-way split inside a single `LICENSE.txt`, sectioned by asset
type: MIT for code, CC BY 4.0 for icons, SIL OFL 1.1 for the font binaries.

> \# Icons: CC BY 4.0 License (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)
>
> The Font Awesome Free download is licensed under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0
> International License and applies to all icons packaged as SVG and JS file types.
>
> \# Fonts: SIL OFL 1.1 License
>
> In the Font Awesome Free download, the SIL OFL license applies to all icons packaged as
> web and desktop font files.

— [FortAwesome/Font-Awesome LICENSE.txt](https://github.com/FortAwesome/Font-Awesome/blob/6.x/LICENSE.txt)

**freeCodeCamp** — demonstrates *directory-scoped* splitting, which is the shape needed
for `sounds/claude/**`:

> The content of this repository is bound by the following licenses:
>
> - The computer software is licensed under the [BSD-3-Clause](LICENSE.md) license.
> - The learning resources in the [`/curriculum`](/curriculum) directory including their
>   subdirectories therein are copyright © 2014 freeCodeCamp.org

— [freeCodeCamp/freeCodeCamp README.md](https://github.com/freeCodeCamp/freeCodeCamp/blob/main/README.md)

**Mechanism for binary files.** Audio files cannot carry a comment header. The
[REUSE Specification](https://reuse.software/spec-3.3/) is the standard answer:

> Each License File MUST be placed in the `LICENSES/` directory in the root of the
> Project. The name of the License File MUST be the SPDX License Identifier of the license
> followed by an appropriate file extension.

and, for files that cannot embed a header:

> the comment header that declares the file's Licensing Information MUST be in an adjacent
> text file of the same name with the additional extension `.license`

REUSE also allows a central `REUSE.toml` mapping globs to licences, which is less noisy
than 15 sidecar files.

### Specifically: projects shipping ElevenLabs audio

Concrete practice exists, though it is individual maintainers' judgement rather than any
authority. Two real repos handle it the way recommended below:

- **`Lithographic/viscriptix-app`** — [`sounds/SOUND_LICENSES.txt`](https://github.com/Lithographic/viscriptix-app/blob/main/sounds/SOUND_LICENSES.txt)
  documents each clip's source, tier, and generation date, then states the terms
  explicitly, including the pass-through: *"Paid subscription plans include commercial
  usage rights / No attribution required for commercial use / Content ownership retained
  by creator / **Cannot be used to train other AI/ML models**"*.
- **`DhvanilPatel/permanent-underclass`** — [`ASSET_LICENSES.md`](https://github.com/DhvanilPatel/permanent-underclass/blob/main/ASSET_LICENSES.md)
  opens with the scoping rule *"Unless explicitly stated otherwise, non-code assets are not
  covered by `LICENSE`"* and carries a per-file table with a Source / License / Attribution
  Required / Status column set. Its ElevenLabs row reads: *"Generated via ElevenLabs (paid
  plan) — ElevenLabs Terms of Service (paid tier — full commercial rights, no attribution
  required)"*.

Both independently arrived at: scope the code licence away from assets, record per-file
provenance including tier, and pass the no-AI-training restriction through to recipients.
That is the pattern.

## 7. Open questions I could not resolve from primary sources

Stating these plainly rather than guessing, per the ticket's instruction.

### 7a. Which plan tier were these 15 clips generated on? — BLOCKING

`ELEVENLABS-VOICE-PROMPT.md` records the model, the Voice Design prompt, stability,
similarity, style, and speed. It does not record the subscription tier. Nothing in the
repo does. This is not answerable from ElevenLabs' documents — only from the account.

It decides everything:

- **Paid at generation time** → commercial use permitted permanently, no attribution
  required, proceed to the recommendation below.
- **Free or signed-out at generation time** → the clips **cannot ship** in a public repo
  that any downstream user may use commercially. Regeneration on a paid plan is mandatory,
  not optional.

**How to settle it:** ElevenLabs account → billing / subscription history, checked against
the clips' generation dates. That record is authoritative.

A *weak* corroborating signal: ElevenLabs began embedding Google DeepMind's SynthID
watermark in "Text to Speech generations by free users" and said they "will expand
coverage to all ElevenLabs audio generations over the coming weeks"
([Detecting audio generated by ElevenLabs with SynthID](https://elevenlabs.io/blog/synthid),
published 2026-06-25, updated 2026-07-22). Given these clips date from July 2026 — inside
that expansion window — a SynthID hit from the
[Audio Detector](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-creative/audio-tools/audio-detector)
proves nothing either way. Use billing history, not the detector.

### 7b. Is Voice Design a "Beta Service"?

ElevenLabs' own product documentation says:

> **Note:** Voice Design is still experimental. Professional Voice Clones offer the highest
> quality and consistency. If you find a PVC that fits your needs, we recommend using it.
> However, the v3 model does not currently support Professional Voice Clones.

— [Voice Design docs](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-creative/voices/voice-design)

The ToS defines Beta Services by designation — services "designated as alpha, beta, pilot,
preview, **or similar designation**" — and excludes them from "Services" entirely
([Beta Services Addendum](https://elevenlabs.io/bsa), referenced from
[ToS §1](https://elevenlabs.io/terms-of-use)). Content generated with Beta Services
"cannot be used for any commercial purpose or in any production environment."

Whether "experimental" in a docs note constitutes a "similar designation" under the BSA is
**not resolvable from the published documents**. It reads as a quality caveat, not a legal
designation, and Voice Design is a billed, generally-listed product with its own pricing
behaviour ("Voice Design only consumes credits when you generate voices"). But the risk is
real and it is not small: if Voice Design is a Beta Service, the entire pack is
non-commercial regardless of tier.

**Recommended action:** ask ElevenLabs support in writing whether Voice Design is a Beta
Service under the BSA, and keep the reply. This is a two-line support ticket that removes
the last material unknown.

The *model* is clear, at least: **Eleven v3 is out of beta.** ElevenLabs announced on
2026-02-02 that "Eleven v3, our most advanced Text to Speech model, is now out of Alpha and
generally available" and is "generally available across all platforms"
([Eleven v3 is Now Generally Available](https://elevenlabs.io/blog/eleven-v3-is-now-generally-available)).
Clips generated after that date are not caught by the BSA on model grounds. Had these been
generated during the v3 alpha window (roughly June 2025 – Feb 2026), they would have been.

### 7c. Post-processing and watermark removal

`ELEVENLABS-VOICE-PROMPT.md` §4 instructs "Trim leading silence hard" and "Normalize the
set". Both are fine. PUP §9(e) prohibits "removing any proprietary rights notices or
markings associated with Output", and ElevenLabs states SynthID watermarks "remain even
when clips are trimmed, sped up, stripped of metadata, or converted into a different file
type" ([SynthID blog](https://elevenlabs.io/blog/synthid)) — so ordinary trimming and
normalisation do not strip anything.

**Do not** add any step that deliberately targets the watermark or C2PA content
credentials. That would move a permitted edit into §9(e) territory.

### 7d. Non-uniqueness of Output

Worth recording, since it bears on how strong a copyright claim the repo can assert over
the clips:

> Due to the nature of machine learning, the Output generated by you using the Services may
> not be unique across users, as the Services may produce the same or similar Output for you
> and a third party. Two different parties may receive the same or similar Output after
> submitting their respective Inputs.

— [ElevenLabs Terms of Service §10](https://elevenlabs.io/terms-of-use)

You own your Output, but you have no exclusivity in it. A one-word clip of the word "Done."
is a thin thing to assert rights over in any case. Practical effect: the asset licence
should be framed as a permission grant plus a pass-through of ElevenLabs' restrictions,
not as an aggressive claim of exclusive ownership.

### 7e. EU users

ElevenLabs maintains separate EEA-facing documents —
[Terms of Use EU](https://elevenlabs.io/terms-of-use-eu) and
[Voice Library Addendum EU](https://elevenlabs.io/vlaeu). This research was conducted
against the non-EEA Terms of Service. If the account is EEA-based, verify §1(c), §4(c) and
§4(a) against the EU text; they are expected to match on these points but that was not
confirmed clause-by-clause.

## RECOMMENDATION

### The audio cannot ship under MIT. Use a split licence.

Ranked, actionable:

---

### Step 0 — Settle the tier before anything else. (Blocking.)

Check ElevenLabs billing history against the clips' generation dates.

- **Free / signed-out?** → **Regenerate on a paid plan.** Do not ship the current files.
  Free-tier output cannot be used commercially, and a public repo that permits commercial
  downstream use is exactly that. Once regenerated, continue to Step 1 — regenerating on
  a paid tier fixes the commercial-use and attribution problems and *nothing else*.
- **Paid?** → Continue to Step 1.

While you are in there, ask support the Voice Design / Beta Services question (§7b) and
record the answer.

**Then: record the answer in `ELEVENLABS-VOICE-PROMPT.md`.** Add the tier and the
generation date alongside the model and settings. The file already claims to be the
"source of truth" for this pack; right now the single most legally load-bearing fact about
those 15 files is the one it omits. Anyone regenerating the pack needs to know they must
be on a paid plan when they do it.

---

### Step 1 — Split the licence. Code MIT, audio under a bespoke asset licence.

**Do not put `sounds/claude/**` under MIT, CC BY, CC BY-SA, or CC0.** All of them breach
PUP §9(n) by granting terms more permissive than your own, and §9(k)/(l) by permitting AI
training.

Concretely:

1. **`LICENSE`** — MIT, as planned, but **scoped**. Add an explicit exclusion line:
   *"This license applies to the software in this repository. It does not apply to the
   audio files in `sounds/claude/`, which are licensed separately — see
   `sounds/claude/LICENSE.md`."*
   Follows the freeCodeCamp directory-scoping pattern.

2. **`sounds/claude/LICENSE.md`** — a bespoke asset licence. It must:
   - state that you own the clips and grant recipients the right to use, copy, redistribute
     and modify them **as part of, or in connection with, this project**;
   - pass through ElevenLabs' restrictions verbatim in substance — **no use as input to
     machine learning or AI training; no inclusion in any dataset for training,
     fine-tuning, testing or improving AI; no use to develop products or services that
     compete with ElevenLabs**; use remains subject to the
     [ElevenLabs Prohibited Use Policy](https://elevenlabs.io/use-policy);
   - record provenance per the viscriptix / permanent-underclass pattern: generated with
     ElevenLabs Voice Design + Eleven v3, on a paid plan, on \<date\>, and link
     `ELEVENLABS-VOICE-PROMPT.md`;
   - if Step 0 lands on free-tier and regeneration somehow does not happen, carry the
     `"elevenlabs.io"` attribution in the release **title**, per §5.

3. **`README.txt`** — one short License section, Twemoji-style. Two sentences:
   code is MIT; the voice clips in `sounds/claude/` are not, and here is why. Someone
   evaluating whether they can vendor this needs to learn that above the fold, not by
   spelunking.

4. **Release zip** — include `sounds/claude/LICENSE.md` in the archive. A licence that
   only exists in the git tree does not travel with a packaged download, and the zip is a
   distribution in its own right.

5. **Optional, cheap:** add REUSE metadata — a `REUSE.toml` glob mapping
   `sounds/claude/**` to the asset licence — so tooling and downstream packagers get the
   split machine-readably rather than having to read prose.

**Be honest in the README that the audio is not open source.** It is redistributable but
not freely licensed. Calling the repo "MIT licensed" full stop would be inaccurate, and
inaccuracy here is the thing that bites a downstream user two years from now.

### Step 2 (optional, later) — If you want the repo to be genuinely, wholly MIT

The only route to a clean, no-asterisks MIT repo is to stop shipping ElevenLabs output.
Two options, in preference order:

- **Ship the recipe, not the audio.** `ELEVENLABS-VOICE-PROMPT.md` is already a complete
  reproduction spec — persona prompt, model, settings, and the exact script for all 15
  lines. Publish it under MIT, have `install.bat` detect a missing `sounds/claude/` and
  point at it, and let each user generate the pack on their own ElevenLabs account under
  their own terms. Zero licensing surface, and it neatly sidesteps §9(n) because you are
  never the one distributing Output. Cost: the voice theme stops working out of the box.
- **Generate locally instead.** The repo already establishes this pattern —
  `tools/New-ChiptuneSounds.ps1` synthesises the chiptune pack with no third-party terms
  attached, which is why the chiptune theme has no licensing question at all. An
  equivalent local-TTS generator (or a permissively-licensed offline TTS model) would put
  the voice pack on the same footing.

Neither is needed to go public. Step 1 is sufficient and is what comparable projects do.
This is the path if "MIT" with no footnote later becomes something worth having.

### Summary answer to the ticket

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Who owns the audio? Differs by tier? | You do. Identical on all tiers ([ToS §4(c)(ii)](https://elevenlabs.io/terms-of-use)). Tier governs *use rights*, not ownership. |
| Redistribute as files in a public repo? | Yes for TTS. The standalone-files prohibition is Sound-Effects-only ([PUP §9(c)](https://elevenlabs.io/use-policy)). |
| Different from use in a published work? | No — [ToS §4(a)](https://elevenlabs.io/terms-of-use) permits off-platform use of downloaded Output without distinguishing the form. |
| Release zip different? | No. Same terms. But ship the asset licence inside the zip. |
| Attribution required? | Free/signed-out: yes, `"elevenlabs.io"` or `"11.ai"` **in the title**. Paid: no ([Help Center](https://help.elevenlabs.io/hc/en-us/articles/13313564601361-Can-I-publish-the-content-I-generate-on-the-platform), [pricing](https://elevenlabs.io/pricing)). |
| Conflicts with MIT? | **Yes — decisively.** [PUP §9(n)](https://elevenlabs.io/use-policy) forbids granting downstream users more permissive terms; §9(j)/(k)/(l) forbid AI training and competing use. |
| Split licence the standard pattern? | Yes — Twemoji, Font Awesome, freeCodeCamp; [REUSE](https://reuse.software/spec-3.3/) for binaries; and real ElevenLabs-shipping repos do exactly this. |
| Ship as-is / separate licence / regenerate? | **Separate asset licence, mandatory.** Regenerate only if Step 0 shows free-tier. A tier upgrade alone never unlocks MIT. |
